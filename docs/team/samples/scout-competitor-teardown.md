# Sample: Scout (Web Research) — Competitor Teardown

> Illustrative teardown of an example competitor. In a real run, every claim below carries a live source link and a date, pulled from the competitor's own site, pricing page, and the public ad libraries.

Here's the read on "Competitor A," based on their live site, pricing page, and what they're currently running in the Meta Ad Library (checked this week).

**Positioning.** They lead with speed: "Get set up in 10 minutes." Their whole promise is low effort and fast time-to-value, aimed squarely at the non-technical buyer who's been burned by clunky enterprise tools. They barely mention depth or power, which tells you who they've decided to lose on purpose.

**Pricing and packaging.** Three tiers: a $29/mo starter, a $79/mo growth tier they visually push as the default, and a "contact us" enterprise tier. The starter is deliberately thin (caps that you hit fast) to funnel people to $79. No free plan, just a 14-day trial with a card required up front, which means they're filtering hard for intent over volume.

**Target customer.** Small teams, 2 to 20 people, non-technical buyer. The testimonials are all founders and ops managers, not engineers.

**Where they're spending.** They're running about a dozen Meta ads right now, almost all video, all hammering the same "10 minutes to set up" angle with a customer-testimonial format. That repetition across many active ads usually signals the angle is working for them. No visible TikTok presence. On search they're bidding on category terms but, notably, not heavily on their own competitor-comparison terms.

**Strengths.** Crisp, single-minded message. Clean onboarding promise. Strong social proof from relatable customers.

**Weaknesses, and our opening.** Their thin starter tier and card-required trial leave a real gap for anyone who wants to try before committing, that's an acquisition wedge. Their "fast and simple" stance means they've ceded "powerful and flexible" entirely, so any buyer who outgrows simple has nowhere to go but out. And they're not defending their comparison search terms, which is cheap ground we could take.

**What I'd do with this:** point our messaging at the depth they abandoned rather than out-simpling the simple play, and have Dex test a low-friction trial offer against their card-required wall.
