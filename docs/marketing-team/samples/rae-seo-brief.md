# Sample: Rae (SEO) — Keyword Strategy + Content Brief

> Illustrative SEO output for an example category. In a real run, the live SERP for each term is checked in the browser before recommending it.

## Keyword strategy

The instinct is to chase the big head term, but it's the wrong target. Here's the cluster view and why.

| Keyword (example) | Intent | Rough volume | Difficulty | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "[category] software" | Commercial | High | Very high | Skip for now. Dominated by incumbents with huge backlink profiles. We can't win this in under a year. |
| "best [category] for small teams" | Commercial | Medium | Medium | **Target.** High intent, our actual buyer, beatable SERP made of listicles we can outdo. |
| "how to [job the product does]" | Informational | Medium | Low-Med | **Target.** Top of funnel, low difficulty, natural place to introduce the product as the answer. |
| "[competitor] alternative" | Commercial | Low | Low | **Target.** Tiny volume, but the highest intent on this list. People searching this are mid-switch. |

The strategy is to build topical authority from the bottom up: win the low-difficulty, high-intent terms first to earn ranking signals, then go after the harder commercial terms once the site has authority. Going straight for the head term is how teams spend six months and rank nowhere.

## Content brief: "best [category] for small teams"

**Target query:** best [category] for small teams
**Search intent:** commercial investigation. The searcher is comparing options and close to deciding. They want an honest, scannable comparison, not a sales page.
**Page type:** comparison/listicle on our blog, updated and dated.
**Suggested H1:** The Best [Category] Tools for Small Teams in [Year]
**Sections to cover:** what small teams should actually look for (sets the criteria in our favor), then 5 to 7 tools each with who it's best for, pricing, and one honest drawback, including ourselves treated fairly. Close with a short decision guide.
**Entities to include:** the main competitors by name, pricing models, the specific jobs small teams need done.
**Internal links:** to our pricing page and to the "how to [job]" article once it exists.
**The unanswered question to nail:** most listicles in this SERP are thin and obviously biased toward whoever wrote them. Win by being genuinely useful and honest about trade-offs, including where we're not the right fit. That's what earns the ranking and the trust.
